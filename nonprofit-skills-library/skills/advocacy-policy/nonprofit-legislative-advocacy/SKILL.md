# Nonprofit Legislative Advocacy

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill for the direct, in-person or written engagement between a nonprofit and
policymakers:

- Preparing an ED, board member, or program staff for a one-on-one meeting with a legislator or
  their staff
- Planning a lobby day or fly-in: scheduling, delegation assignments, meeting logistics
- Writing and rehearsing testimony (written and/or oral) for a legislative committee hearing or
  agency rulemaking hearing
- Building a leave-behind packet (one-pager, fact sheet, ask card) for a legislative visit
- Debriefing after a legislator meeting and logging it for lobbying-hour tracking
- Determining whether a specific activity (a meeting, a mailed letter, a call to a legislator's
  office) counts as "direct lobbying" vs. "grassroots lobbying" under the 501(h) expenditure test,
  and estimating its expenditure allocation

**Boundary — hand off when the ask is:**
- Researching the issue and drafting the underlying brief/position content → `nonprofit-policy-analysis`
  (this skill uses that content in a meeting, it doesn't create it)
- Building a multi-organization coalition's shared ask, MOU, or joint sign-on letter →
  `nonprofit-coalition-building`
- Mobilizing supporters/the public to contact legislators (action alerts, petitions, call-in
  campaigns) → `nonprofit-grassroots-mobilization`
- Structuring the 501(h) election itself, overall annual lobbying budget caps, or (c)(3)/(c)(4) dual
  entity setup → `nonprofit-c3-c4-structure` (this skill applies the limits day-to-day; that skill
  designs the structure and elects/calculates the ceiling)

## Core Framework: The Legislator Meeting

Use the **Ask-Story-Ask** structure for any legislator or staff meeting — state the ask early, prove
it with a constituent story and data, then restate the ask as the close:

1. **Open (2 min):** Introduce the org, thank them for time, state you have a specific ask today.
2. **The ask, stated once clearly (1 min):** "We're asking you to [co-sponsor/vote yes on/oppose]
   [Bill #] because..." — never bury the ask in the middle of the conversation.
3. **The evidence (3-5 min):** One constituent story from their district + one data point from the
   issue brief (pull from `nonprofit-policy-analysis` output). District-specific impact data is the
   single highest-leverage element in these meetings — a national statistic moves fewer legislators
   than "12 of your constituents in [town] use this program."
4. **Handle pushback:** Have the counterargument rebuttal from the brief ready; if the legislator
   raises a fiscal or logistical objection you can't answer, say "let me get back to you" rather than
   guessing — an inaccurate answer damages the relationship more than a follow-up email.
5. **Close with the ask restated + a specific next step:** "Can we count on your yes vote?" / "Will
   your office co-sponsor?" — get a concrete commitment or a stated reason they can't commit yet.
6. **Immediate post-meeting log:** Record date, attendees, ask, response/commitment level (yes / lean
   yes / undecided / lean no / no), and any follow-up owed — this becomes both the org's legislative
   tracking sheet and the lobbying-hour log for 501(h) reporting.

## Lobby Day / Fly-In Planning Checklist

1. Set the date against the legislative calendar (avoid floor-vote days and recess weeks; confirm
   session is in progress)
2. Recruit and assign a delegation lead per meeting (staff, board member, or constituent
   storyteller — mixing a constituent into each meeting significantly increases persuasive impact)
3. Request meetings 3-6 weeks out through the scheduler, not the public comment line
4. Build one shared leave-behind packet: one-pager (from `nonprofit-policy-analysis`), business card,
   org one-pager, specific ask card
5. Run a prep/training session covering the Ask-Story-Ask structure and anticipated pushback
   questions
6. Assign a notetaker per meeting for the debrief log
7. Debrief same-day while memory is fresh; enter commitment levels into the tracking sheet
8. Send personalized thank-you + any promised follow-up materials within 48 hours

## Testimony Preparation

- **Written testimony:** Follow the committee's required format exactly (many committees require
  submission 24-48 hours before the hearing and a specific page/word limit) — expand the issue brief
  into full sentences with a formal opening ("Chair [Name], members of the Committee...").
- **Oral testimony:** Time-box to the committee's limit (often 2-3 minutes) — write to be *heard*,
  not read: short sentences, one clear ask, one memorable story. Rehearse against a stopwatch.
- **Q&A prep:** Anticipate the 3-5 most likely hostile questions from the brief's counterargument
  section and rehearse concise answers; "I don't know, I'll follow up in writing" is an acceptable
  and often better answer than an unsupported guess under oath/on record.
- **Registration logistics:** Confirm the hearing's witness sign-up process and deadline (often
  same-day, first-come or pre-registered) separately for every committee — these rules vary widely
  and missing the window is the most common way an org loses its testimony slot.

## 501(h) Direct vs. Grassroots Lobbying Classification

For 501(c)(3) organizations that have made the 501(h) election, correctly classifying activity
matters because direct and grassroots lobbying draw from separate expenditure ceilings (grassroots
ceiling is set at 25% of the direct lobbying ceiling under the standard formula):

- **Direct lobbying:** Communication with a legislator, legislative staff, or other government
  employee who participates in formulating legislation, that refers to specific legislation and
  reflects a view on it. A legislator meeting, testimony, or a letter to a legislator urging a vote
  all qualify.
- **Grassroots lobbying:** Communication to the general public (not a legislator) that refers to
  specific legislation, reflects a view on it, AND includes a "call to action" (urging the recipient
  to contact a legislator). Missing the call-to-action element means it is not lobbying at all under
  501(h) — this is the most common misclassification in either direction.
- **Not lobbying under 501(h):** Nonpartisan analysis/research/study distributed broadly (this is why
  a well-built issue brief without a call to action, per `nonprofit-policy-analysis`, is generally
  not itself a lobbying expenditure); responding to a written request from a legislative committee;
  self-defense communications about the org's own existence.
- **Practical step:** Log every meeting, testimony appearance, and legislator communication with
  staff time and any material costs allocated to it, and reconcile the total against the org's
  501(h) ceiling quarterly, not just at year-end — year-end reconciliation surprises are the most
  common 501(h) compliance failure.

## Common Failure Modes

- **Burying the ask.** If a legislator's staff leaves a meeting unsure what the org wants them to do,
  the meeting produced no actionable outcome.
- **Bringing national stats with no district data.** Legislators respond most to their own
  constituents' impact; always localize at least one data point.
- **No commitment captured.** A meeting log that says "went well" instead of a specific
  yes/lean-yes/undecided/lean-no/no rating is useless for follow-up strategy.
- **Missing the testimony registration window.** Committees vary widely in sign-up process; confirm
  per-hearing, don't assume last session's process still applies.
- **Misclassifying grassroots vs. direct lobbying.** Counting nonpartisan research as a lobbying
  expenditure (or failing to count a call-to-action mailer as grassroots lobbying) skews the 501(h)
  ceiling calculation and can trigger excise tax exposure if the ceiling is genuinely exceeded.

## Practitioner vs. Advisor Application

- **As the practitioner (ED, policy director, or board member):** Run the Ask-Story-Ask structure
  yourself in the meeting, keep the same-day debrief discipline, and maintain the running 501(h)
  expenditure log rather than reconstructing it at fiscal year-end.
- **As an advisor/consultant:** When coaching a client's team for a first lobby day, run at least one
  full rehearsal per delegation with a hostile-question drill; also do a lightweight lobbying-activity
  audit of the client's planned calendar year activities against the direct/grassroots test before
  the fly-in, since clients new to 501(h) frequently either under-count (risking a real ceiling
  breach) or over-count (avoiding legitimate, non-lobbying nonpartisan education unnecessarily).

## Example

**Input:** "We have a meeting with a state senator's staffer next Tuesday about the behavioral
health bill — help us prep."

**Output approach:** Pull the one-pager and district-specific data from the existing
`nonprofit-policy-analysis` brief; identify a constituent story from that senator's district; script
the Ask-Story-Ask opening and close with a specific ask ("co-sponsor SB 214"); prep 2-3 rebuttals to
likely fiscal objections; assign a notetaker; after the meeting, log the outcome and commitment level
and flag the meeting as direct lobbying for the 501(h) tracking log.
